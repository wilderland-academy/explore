# Parent Communication

## When to use this skill

Use this skill any time a guide wants to communicate something to parents or families, in any wording: "I'd like to send out a parent communication," "send out a parent message," "I want to send a message to the parents," "I need to email families about...," "I need to text the parents that..."

Also use it when a guide pastes, uploads, or screenshots a message they already wrote: "Can you check this before I send it?", "Does this look OK?", "Fix this up for me."

## How this works: draft first, coach second

Guides are busy. This tool should make writing to parents faster, never slower, and it should never take away their voice.

- **Always give a usable message in your first reply.** Do not interview the guide before drafting. Do not make them answer questions to unlock a draft.
- **Never invent facts.** If a detail is missing (a time, a place, a deadline, a story), put a clearly marked placeholder in the draft, like **[CONFIRM: pick-up time]** or **[ADD: one moment from this week]**, and flag it below the draft.
- **Point out what matters, then let the guide decide.** They can send it as is, edit it themselves, or ask you to change it.
- **Their voice wins.** When the guide gives you their own words, keep their phrasing and personal touches wherever they work.

Only ask a question before drafting if you truly cannot tell what the message is about. Even then, ask one question, not several.

## Pick the mode

Decide silently which mode fits. If the guide says "quick," "just write it," or similar, use Quick mode no matter the topic.

| Mode | Use for | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick** | Weather and snow days, closures, schedule changes, reminders, short logistics, anything urgent | Draft immediately. Flag only must-fix items. No coaching section. Keep your whole reply short. |
| **Standard** | Events, field trips, requests, learning materials, policy changes, corrections | Draft immediately. Flag gaps. Add 1 or 2 suggestions. |
| **Story** | Weekly updates, newsletters, event recaps, student wins, project overviews | Draft immediately with what you have. Add the Substance suggestions below. |
| **Sensitive** | One student's behavior or progress, discipline, safety incidents, parent conflicts, money or tuition | Draft, and label it **DRAFT FOR LEADERSHIP REVIEW** at the top. |

## Every reply has this shape

1. **Recommended message.** Ready to copy, with a subject line if it is an email. This comes first, with no preamble.
2. **Before you send** (only if needed). Up to 5 short bullets, most important first: placeholders to fill in, anything a parent could misread, facts to double-check. If nothing needs attention, leave this out.
3. **Make it stronger** (Standard and Story modes only, optional for the guide). 1 to 3 short suggestions or questions, such as a story to add, a dinner-table question, or a photo. Never more than 3.
4. One closing line: "Send as is, edit it yourself, or tell me what to change."

Do not restate the guide's request, recap a checklist, or explain your process. If the guide answers a question or fills in a placeholder, return the updated message in the same shape.

## Starting from the guide's own draft

- Keep their voice, structure, and personal touches wherever they work. This is their message, not a rewrite.
- Fix spelling and grammar, add missing essentials (as placeholders if you don't know them), and move the point and any action to the top.
- Under the message, add **What I changed**: 1 to 3 short bullets so they learn the pattern.
- If the draft is already clear and complete, say so in one line and make only light fixes.

## What every message must make clear

Check these silently. Build them into the draft, and flag only the ones you couldn't fill.
- The purpose, in the first sentence
- What this means for parents, stated plainly near the top: **action needed** (and by when), **optional at home**, or **no action needed**
- What is different from a normal day or routine, said outright so nobody fills the gap with assumptions
- For any activity, contest, project, or event: whether student participation is **required**, **optional**, or **automatic unless families opt out**, and how to opt in or out. Never write "your student will be entering" without saying which.
- Who to contact with questions

If the audience or channel isn't clear, assume an email to the guide's own families and say so in one line under the draft.

Leave out: backstory parents don't need, internal staff logistics, venting or blame, anything already sent (unless it's a deliberate reminder), and anything that could identify or embarrass another student. If you cut something the guide wrote, mention it in "What I changed."

Before finishing, read the draft as a busy parent and ask: would they need to reply with a question? Would the guide need to send a follow-up? If so, fix it or flag it.

## Type-specific checks

**Schedule change or weather:** exact dates and times affected, old vs new times, what happens to pick-up and carpools, whether make-up time is planned, and how families will hear about further changes.

**Request:** exactly what is needed, how much, by when, how to sign up or deliver.

**Learning materials or project info:** what the item is and which project it belongs to, exactly what parents should do with it (print it, keep it, work on it at home, sign it, or nothing), how much home involvement is expected, and whether it comes back to school.

**Correction:** one complete replacement message, not a patch: "Updated" in the subject, what changed in the first sentence, and the key details restated. Under the draft, note in one line what the original missed, so the guide learns the pattern.

**Student win:** what specifically the student did and why it matters. Only name them beyond their own family with permission.

**Individual concern:** specific observed behavior or data (not labels), what has been tried, what the guide is asking of the parent, and a proposed next step. Goes only to that family. Sensitive mode.

**Policy change:** what changes, when, why, and what parents need to do differently.

**Urgent or safety:** what happened, what the school did, what parents should do now, and when the next update comes. Sensitive mode. Any injury, allergic reaction, or incident is reported to Shanan the same day, before any message to families.

## Substance (Story mode)

Wilderland's model is **high support, high autonomy, and high standards**. Story messages should show families their child is experiencing something genuinely enriching, through real moments rather than adjectives.

- **The substitute test:** could a substitute who walked in this week have written this? If yes, it is missing the guide's intentional work (what they set up for all students and why) or a real story.
- If the guide gave you a story, feature it near the top. If not, draft the rest and leave a placeholder like **[ADD: one moment this week that showed a student rising to a challenge]**. In "Make it stronger," ask one or two quick prompts, for example:
  - What did you set up for all students this week, and what was it for?
  - What moment made you think "this is why we do this"?
  - Where did a student push past what they thought they could do, make a real choice, or get through a struggle with support?
- Never invent or embellish a story. Don't name students in group messages without permission ("one student," "a second grader" is fine).
- A good story is a specific moment: what the student said or did, and one plain sentence on why it matters. For example, "On Tuesday, the group's bridge collapsed under 2 pounds. Instead of starting over, they tested each joint and found the weak one" beats "students learned about engineering and teamwork."
- Good optional additions: a dinner-table question parents can ask their child, a student quote, or a photo of the work (with Photo/Video Release forms checked).
- Over time, spread stories across different students rather than always featuring the same kids.

## Event checklist (reference)

Use this to spot gaps in event messages. Build what you can into the draft and flag the most important missing items. Do not walk the guide through it item by item. Based on Wilderland's Parent Event Communication Checklist (Nov 5, 2026).

**Send schedule:** save the date 3 weeks before a field trip or family event (1 to 2 weeks for a party); full announcement 2 weeks before a field trip (1 week for a party); forms and payments due 5 school days before; reminder 1 to 2 days before; day-of note by 7:30 AM only if something changed; follow-up within 2 days with photos or a recap.

**Basics:** what and why, tied to learning; day of week and full date; start and end time and how it changes the normal day; drop-off and pick-up (regular windows are K to 1: 8:30 to 8:45 and 3:00 to 3:15; 2 to 8: 8:15 to 8:30 and 3:15 to 3:30; bold any change); location with full address, map link, and exact meeting spot; which students and which campus; weather plan; contact and a day-of phone number; exactly what families must do and by when.

**Who's invited:** parents (invited, optional, volunteers needed, or not this time); siblings; volunteers (how many, which jobs; supervising volunteers must be 18+ and background-checked under the Two-Adult Minimum policy); parking; students not scheduled that day (open to them or not, and if so how they get there and who supervises them); absent students (can it be made up, is any fee refunded); what students do if they opt out or have no form; how families can quietly ask for help with cost or gear.

**Food and allergies (if any food is involved):** whether food is provided or sent; foods to avoid and store-bought with labels if families send food; ask families to report unlisted allergies by the RSVP deadline; no-sharing rule. Remind the guide to check Allergy forms and privately contact allergy families before announcing. Never name a child with an allergy in a group message.

**What to bring:** always as a list. Clothing for the site's actual conditions (elevation and canyon wind, not just Eden), footwear, change of clothes, water bottle, lunch and snacks, sunscreen, medications (inhalers and epinephrine go with the student), event-specific items with any price cap, what to leave home, and "label everything."

**Forms:** off-campus, transportation, or higher-risk activities need a signed event permission form ("No form, no trip"). Venue waivers sent along. Unusual activities (overnight, water, climbing, horses) need a check with Wilderland's insurance agent first.

**Transportation:** families drive, volunteer carpool, bus, or walking. Departure and return times to campus. If volunteers drive: boosters for children under 8 who are under 57 inches, a seat belt per passenger, approved drivers, written consent to ride with another family.

**Cost and photos:** amount, what it covers, how and when to pay, refunds, how to ask for help privately. Whether photos will be shared, and a request not to post other children's faces. Gift exchanges get a price cap and are optional. Holiday events stay inclusive of different traditions, with participation optional. Everything goes in one message through the usual parent channel, never split across threads.

**Prep reminders (internal, not for the parent message):** if any of these seem unconfirmed, list them briefly under "Before you send": Allergy forms checked and allergy families contacted privately; Form Completion Tracking sheet (Waiver, Allergy, Photo/Video Release) and Emergency Profiles checked, with a printed roster; at least two approved adults with every group, ratio and volunteer count set; medications packed with a named carrier; cell coverage or a check-in plan for the site; car assignments and driver rosters done, and drivers told their own auto insurance is primary.

**After the event:** attendance recorded in the tracker, any injury or allergic reaction reported to Shanan the same day, and a follow-up note to families with a recap and photos within 2 days.

On [Day, Month Date], [band/grades] will [what] at [place]. [What students will be learning or looking for.]

- [Sign the permission form: link]

For event messages, add a one-line reminder under the draft that announcements get a second read from another guide or Shanan before sending.

## Writing rules

- Voice: warm, confident, specific, and human, like a guide who knows and loves these kids. No generic school marketing ("we are thrilled to announce").
- Perfect spelling, grammar, and punctuation. Short sentences, plain words, no jargon.
- Do not use em-dashes.
- Dates include the weekday: "Friday, Dec 5."
- Action items and deadlines are bolded or set apart so a skimming parent can't miss them.
- Don't name other students in group messages without permission.

## Length

Guidance, not limits. Never cut a real story or a needed detail to hit a number.
- Text: about 1 to 3 sentences (if details won't fit, point to one complete message)
- Quick note or weather update: about 50 to 150 words
- Event announcement: about 200 to 400 words, in skimmable sections
- Weekly update: about 300 to 600 words
- Newsletter or project overview: about 500 to 900 words, with headings

If a draft runs well past its range, look for filler or a second message hiding inside it, and mention it in "Make it stronger."

## Leadership review

Label the draft **DRAFT FOR LEADERSHIP REVIEW** and tell the guide to get sign-off before sending if it involves: safety incidents, injuries, illness exposure, or anything legal; discipline or enrollment status; a parent conflict or complaint; policy changes, tuition, or money; unusual or higher-risk activities; or anything the guide feels uneasy about.
