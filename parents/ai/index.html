Mr. Fox and your child's safety

Students in grades 2 to 8 can get help from Mr. Fox, an AI coach, on their Life Skills challenges, Life Skill Labs and passion projects, and with working out a conflict. Here is exactly what it does, what it won't do, and how we keep an eye on it.

What it is

  • An AI chat inside the student website, built on Anthropic's Claude models. It tells students it is an AI, not a person, every time they open it.
  • It is a coach, not an answer machine. It asks questions and gives hints, and it does not do the work for students. Research on AI tutors found students who were handed answers learned less, while a tutor built to guide them avoided that harm.
  • Why have it at all? It is there whenever a student is stuck, including when every guide is helping someone else, and it can talk about almost any subject a challenge or project touches. Guides still pass every challenge, and anything big, risky or personal goes to a real adult.
  • It helps students find and do challenges, Labs and projects, work through a conflict, and learn to use AI well. It shows up as a fox emoji, but it has no name or personality, and it does not act as a friend or companion.
  • Conflicts, the Wilderland way. The coach walks a student through our Behavior Policy's steps: calm down first, describe what happened without labels, name feelings and needs, see the other side, plan a calm private talk ("When I saw…, I felt…, because I need…, would you be willing…?"), and own their part. It never takes sides. When someone is unsafe, it tells the student to get an adult right away, and staff are alerted.
  • Not academics, for now. AI that hands out answers makes it easy to skip the thinking. The coach tells students it doesn't help with schoolwork. We're trying a hint-only academics coach with a few students first, chosen with their families. In that pilot it never gives answers or helps on tests, and staff are alerted and read every academics conversation.
  • Kindergarten and 1st grade don't use it. Their challenges are guide-led.

The safeguards

Every message is checked twice

Before the coach answers, a separate AI screens the student's message for safety concerns. Before the student sees the answer, another check reviews the coach's reply. If either check can't run, the coach doesn't answer.

Topics it won't touch

Anything sexual or romantic, violence and weapons, drugs, alcohol and vaping, self-harm, scary or graphic content, hate, and getting around rules or filters. It does not give medical, mental-health or legal advice, and it stays out of politics and religion.

Serious messages reach a person

If a child mentions hurting themselves, being hurt, or hurting someone, the AI does not reply in its own words. The child sees a fixed message pointing them to a trusted adult, SafeUT and 988, and our staff get an alert right away.

Guides can read every chat

Every conversation is saved, and every staff member can read them. Students are told this up front. Students can't delete their chats or change what was said.

Personal details stay out

The coach never asks for personal information. Email addresses, phone numbers, street addresses and links are removed from a message before it is saved or sent to the AI, and the student is reminded to keep those private.

Safety comes first

For fire, tools, cooking, water, heights or anything risky, the coach says an adult must be there and the guide's safety check comes first. It never explains how to skip safety steps.

Limits on use

Students can send up to 50 messages a day, and each conversation has a set length. Long sessions tend to drift away from learning, so the coach nudges students to take a break.

It teaches good AI habits

The first chat is a short lesson: what AI is, that it can be wrong, why you never share personal details, and how to ask a good question. Short tips about using AI well keep coming up after that.

Your child's data

  • What we keep: the messages your child sends, the coach's replies, any safety flags, and how many messages they sent each day. We store these in Wilderland's own Google Firebase database, tied to their school account.
  • Who can see it: your child, and Wilderland staff. It is used only for your child's learning and safety, never for advertising.
  • The AI company: messages go to Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, to get the coach's reply. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, these messages aren't used to train its models, and Anthropic deletes them within 30 days unless it needs them to enforce its usage policy or the law requires it.
  • Your choices: you can ask to read your child's conversations, ask us to delete them, or ask us to turn the coach off for your child. Talk to your child's guide or email the school.

Wilderland follows Anthropic's requirements for organizations that serve minors (content screening, monitoring and reporting, teaching safe use, and telling users they are talking to an AI), and we built the coach to protect children's privacy under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

If your child is struggling

Mr. Fox is not a counselor. If your child is having a hard time, please reach out to their guide. Utah families can also use the free SafeUT app (chat with a licensed counselor 24/7) or call or text 988. In an emergency, call 911.
